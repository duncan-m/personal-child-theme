Across hundreds or thousands of card payments, the revenue number can look reassuring. What it doesn’t show is how much of that money still carries unresolved exposure.

Some payments may later be disputed or charged back. The useful question for finance is where that exposure sits, how it changes over time, and what the business should do about it.

The practical takeaway

Track recent card revenue by risk segment. Review where exposure is concentrated. Use that view to inform growth, authentication and acceptance decisions.

Why approved payments still carry exposure

A successful payment tells you that a transaction has gone through. It does not tell you that the business has passed every opportunity for a later dispute. When a chargeback is raised, the disputed funds can be debited while the case is resolved.1

Reporting total sales and recording losses when they arrive leaves a gap between today’s growth and tomorrow’s outcomes. Alongside normal revenue reporting, finance needs a view of the payments that remain exposed.

That is a rolling view: new transactions enter it, older transactions mature, and disputes reveal how earlier groups of payments actually performed.

Age is a useful lens, not a universal expiry date. Disputes can often be initiated within 120 days, with longer periods in some circumstances. For future services, timing can run from the service or event rather than the payment. The 90+ day bucket below does not mean risk-free.1

Start with customer tenure and transaction age

A practical first cut is to group payments by how long the customer has been with you and how recently the transaction took place. These two dimensions turn a single revenue total into a view you can investigate.

Newer customers
Less history to assess. Look closely at early payment behaviour, subsequent disputes and the quality of newly acquired revenue.
Established customers
More history to work with. Use repeat payment behaviour and past outcomes to test whether these customers carry lower exposure.

Refine the picture with region and liability

Customer tenure and transaction age give you a starting point. Add issuing region and actual liability status to see whether apparently similar payments carry different exposure.

Issuing region
Compare fraud and dispute outcomes by the country or region in which the card was issued. Avoid assuming that one market’s performance applies everywhere.Look for: segments whose observed loss or dispute patterns differ from the portfolio.
Liability status
Record whether liability actually shifted, rather than treating “3DS used” as blanket protection. A qualifying shift can protect against certain fraud disputes; other dispute reasons can remain exposed.2Look for: the exposure the merchant still carries after authentication.

The result is a view of revenue by risk segment: which payments are recent, which customer groups they come from, and where the merchant still carries liability.

Keep different risks distinct. Track acquirer or payment-provider concentration, unsettled balances and reserves alongside this analysis. They are a separate layer from customer dispute risk.

Four actions for finance and payments teams

The value is in the decisions this view enables. Start with a small, repeatable report and give each review a clear outcome.

  1. Build a view of exposed revenue

    Bring recent successful payments into one view, grouped by transaction age and customer tenure. Add issuing region and liability status where the data is available, and keep unknown values visible.

    Show payment value and transaction count alongside later disputes and losses. Keep the amount exposed separate from any estimate of how much you expect to lose.

    Start hereAsk for one export linking payment date, value, customer tenure, issuing country, liability status and any subsequent dispute outcome.

  2. Find where exposure is concentrated

    Review which segments account for the most payment value, how that mix is changing, and how earlier groups of payments performed once they had time to mature.

    Compare acquisition sources and returning customers. A rise in sales is more useful when you can see the quality of the revenue behind it.

    Start hereAdd a regular summary of the largest exposure concentrations, the changes since the last review and the questions that need investigation.

  3. Turn the findings into decisions

    Bring finance, growth, fraud and payments together around the same segments. Use the evidence to decide whether to change authentication, acceptance rules, routing, onboarding or acquisition spend.

    Evaluate conversion, losses and customer friction together. Compare acquisition with retention using what the business ultimately keeps from each group.

    Start hereChoose one segment to investigate. Agree a specific change, an owner and a review date, with conversion and loss measures defined before it starts.

  4. Measure what the business keeps

    Follow each group of payments through to later outcomes. Review subsequent losses, recoveries and relevant fees alongside the original sales and conversion figures.

    Use the findings to improve the risk buckets and the decisions built on them. Finance, growth and fraud should be able to explain performance using the same numbers.

    Start hereAgree a shared measure of retained value, document what it includes, and review mature groups of payments against the assumptions made when they were approved.
For the next revenue review

Bring three questions to the table

  • Where is our recent card revenue concentrated, and how much sits in higher-risk segments?
  • What did earlier groups of similar payments actually lose once disputes arrived?
  • What decision will we make differently because we now have this view?

Revenue growth is more useful when you can see the exposure that comes with it.
